B. BASE PAPER AND REPOSITORY
======================================================================

Paper: “DiffCR: A Fast Conditional Diffusion Framework for Cloud Removal
From Optical Satellite Images” IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and
Remote Sensing, 2024.

Repository: https://github.com/XavierJiezou/DiffCR

The user has already implemented and trained DiffCR. The existing model
and checkpoint are valuable and must be preserved.

Paper method, in brief: - Paired cloudy and clear satellite images are
used for supervised learning. - The cloudy image is the conditioning
input. - The clear image is the reconstruction target. - Diffusion
training uses noisy versions of the clear target conditioned on the
cloudy image. - The DiffCR model predicts the clean image x0 directly
(data prediction), rather than only predicting noise. - The architecture
includes a condition encoder, time encoder, and denoising autoencoder
with TCFBlocks. - The paper discusses patch-based training. SEN12MS-CR
is described with 256x256 patches and 50% overlap.

Important: The paper does not mandate a separate cloud-mask network,
cloud mask as a required model input, SIFT/RANSAC registration, or
Pix2Pix/PatchGAN. Those are not mandatory DiffCR stages.

The uploaded paper is referenced in the project conversation as
turn25file0 (14 pages). Useful locations noted: - Fig. 1 and Section
III, pp. 2–4: cloudy conditioning input, noisy clear target y_t, model
predicts clean y_0. - Section III-D, p. 7: direct data prediction y0
loss. - Section IV-A, p. 7: datasets; SEN12MS-CR uses 256x256 patches
with 50% spatial overlap.

======================================================================
N. ALIGNMENT: DO NOT CONFUSE THE STAGES
======================================================================

Four separate concepts: 1. Geographic overlap: do both scenes cover a
common geographic area? 2. Reprojection/grid matching: are they in the
same CRS, dimensions, and pixel grid? 3. Pixel-level registration: do
the same ground features occupy corresponding pixels? 4. Common valid
footprint: are both images valid at the same locations?

Matching CRS, dimensions, and affine transforms proves that the images
occupy a common output grid. It does not independently prove exact
pixel-level correspondence.

The user explicitly corrected this distinction: overlap and alignment
are different. Preserve that distinction in all future explanations.

Why it matters: DiffCR is trained using paired cloudy/clear imagery and
a pixel-wise reconstruction target. Misregistered pairs can corrupt
supervision, even if their geospatial metadata match.
